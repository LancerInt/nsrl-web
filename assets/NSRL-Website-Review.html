Website content, imagery & design review

NSRL: a strong design foundation, with clearer evidence and imagery needed.

Reviewed 21 October 2026 · 21 public pages extracted · Homepage, Research and Contact visually inspected in a desktop browser.

Recommendation

Keep the navy-and-white editorial direction. Prioritise content accuracy, relevant scientific images and a dependable proposal enquiry flow before adding more animation or decorative effects.

The serif headings, simple navigation, restrained blue accents and prominent proposal button suit a specialist contract research organisation. The sector and division structure is useful, and the searchable research catalogue gives technical visitors a direct route to relevant work.

This is a content and desktop design review. It is not a certificate validation, scientific-method audit, mobile-device test, accessibility certification, security assessment or measured performance report. No enquiries were sent. Certificate links were inventoried, but their contents, validity and scope were not independently verified.

What the website says

NSRL presents itself as a Salem-based preclinical contract research laboratory serving agrochemicals, industrial chemicals and food and feed additives.

  • Seven divisions: Toxicology, Ecotoxicology, Chemistry, Microbiology, Entomology, Vivarium and Custom Studies.
  • Three study-package groups: Agrochemical Registration Dossier, REACH & Chemical Compliance, and Food & Feed Safety Panel.
  • Published headline claims: 20,000+ GLP studies, 275+ clients, 50+ countries, 150,000 sq ft, 450+ instruments, 350+ professionals and 70+ clean rooms.
  • Certificate/recognition records: OECD GLP, NABL ISO/IEC 17025:2017, SIRO/DSIR and CIBRC.

These are extracted statements from the site, not independently confirmed facts. The accompanying text archive contains the main content of all 21 pages; the JSON also retains image attributes and discovered links.

Prioritised findings

P1: resolve before relying on the site for sponsor enquiries. P2: strengthen clarity and credibility. P3: presentation consistency.

PriorityObserved issue & evidenceRecommended change
P1Contradictory site count

Careers says “Three sites · Tamil Nadu”; the same section and Facilities describe one site.

Confirm the real facility structure and address against operating records. Update Careers, About, Facilities and the footer from one shared location record.
P1Proposal flow depends on an email application

Contact explicitly says the form only drafts an email; nothing is submitted. Careers uses the same approach.

Add reliable server-side delivery with an on-page receipt, failure state and spam protection. Keep direct email as a fallback. Ask initially for name, work email, company, sector and a short study brief; make phone optional.
P1Species counters count descriptions or groups

Entomology shows “Species 1”, but the entry is “Agriculturally and economically important pest species”. Ecotoxicology combines a 26-species headline with “Species 12” and a list of organism groups.

Use an approved species register. Until available, remove Entomology’s counter and label the ecotoxicology list “Organism groups”. Distinguish named species, groups and actual available test models.
P1Evidence is weaker than the certainty of some claims

“India’s largest”, “20,000+ GLP studies”, 350+ professionals, 450+ instruments, 70+ clean rooms, FDA/EMA acceptance and the first nano-fertiliser registration are asserted. These are site claims, not independently verified findings.

Assign an internal evidence owner and as-of date to each claim. Retain only supported numbers and superlatives; obtain approved wording for any regulatory acceptance statement.
P1Accreditation labels are too broad

The homepage totals four “Accreditations”, while the records include GLP certification, NABL accreditation, SIRO recognition and CIBRC recognition. Research assigns badges to divisions.

Use “Accreditations & recognitions” for the collective heading. Show exact certificate number, issuing authority, validity, address and scope. Match method-level badges to the current certificate scope, rather than suggesting blanket coverage.
P2Food imagery does not communicate the sector

Food & Feed Additives reuses the bacterial petri-dish image also used for Microbiology.

Use the generated grain, feed, additive and oil testing image for the sector; keep microbiology imagery for microbiology.
P2Division photography is repetitive and weakly specific

Ecotoxicology uses greenhouse seedlings; Entomology uses a generic compound microscope; Vivarium uses a laboratory team image.

Use aquatic testing for Ecotoxicology, insect observation for Entomology and histopathology for Toxicology. Use approved real animal-housing photography for Vivarium.
P2The central message is repeated too often

One laboratory, one campus, one archive and one chain of custody recur across home, About, Services, Facilities, packages and division footers.

Keep this positioning in the hero and one explanatory section. Replace repetitions with sponsor information: sample needs, methods, deliverables, study dependencies and quotation inputs.
P2Several headings explain the page instead of the service

Examples include “Seven divisions, set out in full”, “What the ledger says”, “An assembly, not an invention” and “The catalogue does not change”.

Use descriptive headings such as “Preclinical research divisions”, “Laboratory experience” and “Regulatory study packages”. Preserve the editorial tone in supporting copy.
P2Research has consecutive conversion sections

Research ends with “Not sure which division owns your question?” followed immediately by the shared “Tell us the authority you need to satisfy.” block.

Keep one useful final CTA per page. Give it a relevant action such as “Discuss your study requirements”.
P2Old and incomplete news weakens the launch impression

The archive contains two 2023 records; one has no article and is visibly marked “No note filed”.

Remove incomplete entries from the public listing or provide an approved complete article. Publish current capability, team or conference updates with dates and named owners.
P2Method names and framework groupings need technical editing

An isolated “OECD 218” appears without its method title. Counts such as “methods”, “platforms” and “frameworks” combine unlike categories.

Have the scientific owner approve each method title, guideline identifier and grouping. Display a complete method name beside every guideline. Do not infer a regulatory claim from a badge or a raw array length.
P3Numeric formatting and terminology vary

International pages mix “1,50,000 sq ft” with “150,000 sq ft”; Careers uses Eco-toxicology while Research uses Ecotoxicology.

Use 150,000 sq ft for the international site and adopt one terminology list. Keep units, capitalisation and model names consistent.

Sections that need genuine photography

  • About / Leadership / Careers: a real NSRL team photograph and an approved leadership portrait.
  • Facilities: exterior or reception, instrument benches, environmental test systems, archive and controlled rooms, each with a truthful caption.
  • Vivarium: approved animal-housing and welfare photography with accurate context.
  • Custom Studies: a genuine study-design meeting, or use a clean text-led header rather than repeating the generic scientist image.
  • Contact, Resources and study-package catalogue: retain useful typography, maps, certificate previews and structured tables; extra decorative photos are unnecessary.

Design changes worth making

  1. Shorten the homepage. Use hero → verified proof points and certificates → three sectors → seven divisions → real facility evidence → one proposal CTA. Merge the repeated single-laboratory explanations.
  2. Improve the hero crop. Put the instrument detail on the right and white HTML text over a quiet navy area on the left. Keep essential instrument details clear of the heading. On mobile, stack the copy above the image or supply a separate crop.
  3. Make scientific divisions visually distinct. Use one relevant image per division, backed by capability lists. Keep the Research overview compact and searchable rather than adding seven large banners to it.
  4. Use restrained accents. Navy for primary actions and footer, cool white for reading, pale blue for grouping and a single teal/blue accent for links and focus states. Keep text and buttons outside the image files.
  5. Reduce ornamental data. Retain counters a sponsor can interpret and verify. Remove counts of generic “families” or “frameworks” where they add decoration but little decision value.
  6. Make certificates easier to verify. Add a consistent certificate preview, issuer, identifier, scope and validity date, followed by an accessible PDF link. Preserve the existing category distinctions.

Copy direction

Keep the existing hero “Complete preclinical research, start to finish.” Simplify the supporting line to: “Chemistry, toxicology and environmental testing for agrochemical, industrial chemical and food & feed submissions.” Use only after NSRL approves the service scope.

Replace “Seven divisions, set out in full” with “Explore our scientific divisions”. Replace the contact headline with “Discuss your study requirements”. Replace “Tell us the authority you need to satisfy” with “Plan your next regulatory study”.

Implementation and verification checklist

  • Give each replacement a distinct filename and descriptive alt text that matches its actual subject. Keep image dimensions reserved to prevent layout shifts.
  • Export responsive WebP/AVIF derivatives; load the hero promptly and lazy-load images below the fold. Set accurate responsive image sizes. Confirm actual transfer sizes rather than assuming the HTML fallback URL is what every browser downloads.
  • In an initial full-page browser capture, lower facility images appeared as empty reserved areas. That may be lazy loading. Scroll them into view and confirm they load before treating this as a production fault.
  • Verify 360, 390, 768 and desktop widths; check hero cropping, mobile menus, wide tables, keyboard focus, contrast and reduced-motion behaviour. These checks remain to be done.
  • Test a real enquiry end to end after a delivery service exists: valid submission, invalid input, failure handling, duplicate submission and receipt. Do the same for Careers.
  • Check production metadata, canonical domain, sitemap, indexing settings and social previews before launch. Existing HTML already contains page-specific titles; this review does not claim that all SEO configuration is missing.
  • Review site claims and the method catalogue with the scientific/quality owner. Obtain approval before changing technical wording or certification coverage.
